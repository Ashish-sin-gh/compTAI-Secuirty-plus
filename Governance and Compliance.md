# Governance and Compliance
## Governance
- Overall management of Organizations IT infrastructure, policies, procedures, operations.
- Involve establishing a Framework that align with org's requirement and regulatory requirements. 
	- Rules
	- Responsibility 
	- Practices 
	For IT.
- Governance effect the creating of 
	- guidance
	- policies
	- standards
	- procedures
- Governance is useful for :
	- **Risk Management :**
		- Identify, assess and manage potential risks that could impact organizations security.
	- **Strategic alignment :** 
		- Org's IT strategy algin with its business requirements.
	- **Resource Management :**
		- Efficient and effective use of IT resources.
	- **Performance measurement :**
		- Mechanism for **monitoring** and **revisions** IT performance processes.
		- for framework's improvement in gaps, weakness that might have arrived due to changes in tech, regulation, industry culture.
#### Governance Structures(Components)
1. **Boards:**
	- Board of directors
	- grp of individual, elected by shareholder to oversee the management of an Organization.
	- Responsible for setting the org's 
		- strategic direction, 
		- establishing policies, 
		- making significate decision
2. **Committees**
	- Subgroups of a board of directors 
	- Each with a specific focus.
	- Example:
		- auditing 
		- Cybersecurity
3. **Govt Entities**
	- Governance of public / regulated organization.
	- Establish laws and regulation org must comply with.
4. **Centralized Structure**
	- Decision making - at top level only
5. **De-centralized Structure**
	- Distribute decision making through out the organization.
#### Policies
###### Acceptable Use Policy (AUP)
- Outline dos and don'ts for users while interacting with IT systems and resources.
###### Information Security Policies 
- Outline how an org protects information assets from threats, both internal and external.
	- Data Classification
	- Access Control
	- Encryption
	- Physical Security 
###### Business Continuity Policies
- Focus on how the org will continue with its critical operation during or after a disruption.
- Minimal interruption to services 
- Recover as quickly as possible
###### Disaster Recovery Policies
- Focus on how the org will recover its IT system and data after a disaster.
- Steps for data backup and restorations.
- Hardware and software recovery
- Alternative processing locations 
###### Incident response policy
- A plan for handling security incidents.
###### Software Development Life Cycle (HDLC) policy
- how S/W is developed within an organization.
- initial requirement gathering
	- design
	- coding
	- testing
	- Deployment
	- maintenance 
- ways to Secure coding practices 
- Code review methods
- ways to test s/w
###### Change Management Policy 
- How changes to the IT system and processes are handled.
- in Control and corradiated manner 
#### Standards
- Provide framework for implementing security measures.
- address all security posture of the org.
	- **Password standards**
		- resistance against brut force attacks
		- robustness 
	- **Access control Standards**
		- Who has access to what resources within an org
		- Models:
			- **Discretionary Access Control (DAC)**
				- owner of a resource decides who can access it
			- **mandatory Access Control (MAC)**
				- labels or classification  used to determine access
				- System/admin enforces rules
			- **Role Based Access Control (RBAC)**
				- Assign access based on role.
		- **Principle of least privilege**
		- **Separation of duties**
	- **Physical security standards**
		- parameter security
		- access control mechanism (biometric, keycards)
		- Fire suppression control
		- HVAC controls
		- Power redundancy system
	- **Encryption Standards**
		- AES often used for data at Rest
		- RSA for secure communication 
#### Procedure 
- Systematic sequence of actions or steps taken to achieve a specific outcome.
- Ensure consistence, efficiency and compliance with standards 
- Example
	- Emergency evacuation procedure.
	- Data backup Procedure
- **Change Management procedure**
	- dealing with changes within the organization.
	- Example
		- implementing new software system
		- Restructuring the org
	- Goal :
		- Change take place smoothly
		- without any disruption to system and operations.
	- Stages :
		1. need for change is identified and potential impact is assessed
		2. A plan is developed 
		3. Change is implemented in stages 
		4. Review is conducted.
	- **Test the changes first**
	- **Should have a backup plan for rollback**
	- Designated maintenance hour
	- Record and review for future changes.
- **Onboarding and offboarding Procedure**
	- **Onboarding**
		- The process of integrating new employee into the org.
		- Orientation and Training.
		- goal :
			- new employee are productive and engaged as quickly as possible.
	- **Offboarding**
		- The process of managing the transition when employee leaves
		- Retrieving company property 
		- Disabling access to the systems
		- Conducting exit interviews
- **Playbooks**
	- **Checklist of actions** to perform when need to detect and respond to incident arise. 
	- Example
		- Responding to cybersecurity incident 
		- Handling customer complaint.
